Account Access and Login Troubleshooting Guide

Document owner: Account Support. Version 4.0. Last reviewed 2026-02-18.

Scope

This guide covers login failures, password resets, multi-factor authentication problems and account lockouts. It does not cover billing questions or suspected account compromise; suspected compromise follows the Security Escalation procedure instead.

Login Failure Triage

Work through these causes in order. Most login failures resolve at step one or two, and jumping straight to a password reset hides the real cause.

  1. Confirm the customer is using the correct account email. Customers with both a personal and a work account frequently attempt the wrong one.
  2. Check whether the account is locked. Five consecutive failed attempts trigger a 30-minute automatic lockout.
  3. Check whether the account is suspended for non-payment. A suspended account rejects valid credentials without saying why.
  4. Check the multi-factor authentication status. A replaced phone is the single most common cause of a login failure that looks like a wrong password.
  5. Only then offer a password reset.

Password Reset Procedure

Password resets are self-service. Support staff never set a password on a customer's behalf and never send a password over any channel.

  1. Verify the customer's identity against the account email and one additional factor from the account record.
  2. Trigger the reset email from the admin console. Do not read the reset link aloud or paste it into the ticket.
  3. The reset link is valid for 60 minutes and can be used once.
  4. If the customer does not receive the email, check the suppression list before re-sending. Repeated sends to a suppressed address will never arrive.

Account Lockout

Automatic lockouts clear after 30 minutes. An agent may clear a lockout early after identity verification. A lockout that recurs immediately after being cleared indicates automated credential-stuffing against the account and must be raised as a security escalation rather than cleared again.

Multi-Factor Authentication Recovery

A customer who has lost their MFA device must complete identity verification before MFA is reset. MFA reset is a high-risk action: it requires approval from an account support lead, and it is recorded in the account audit log. Never reset MFA on the basis of an email request alone.

Account Closure

Account deletion is irreversible and removes customer data within 30 days. It always requires explicit human approval and an explicit confirmation from the account owner, not merely from a user on the account. An automated agent may prepare a closure request but may never execute one.
